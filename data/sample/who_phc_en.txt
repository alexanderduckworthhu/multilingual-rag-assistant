WHO guidance excerpt: Primary health care (educational sample for RAG portfolio)

Primary health care (PHC) is a whole-of-society approach to health that aims to
deliver equitable health services as close as feasible to everyday environments.
PHC includes three components: integrated health services with an emphasis on
primary care and public health functions; multisectoral policy and action; and
empowered people and communities.

WHO identifies PHC as the most inclusive, equitable, and cost-effective way to
achieve universal health coverage and the health-related Sustainable Development
Goals. Countries strengthening PHC typically invest in community health workers,
essential medicines, laboratory capacity at the first level of care, referral
systems, and digital tools that support continuity of care.

Quality primary care is continuous, comprehensive, coordinated, and people-centred.
It addresses physical and mental health needs across the life course. During
pandemics and other emergencies, strong PHC platforms enable early detection,
risk communication, vaccination delivery, and maintenance of essential services.

Barriers to PHC include underfunding, workforce shortages, fragmented vertical
programmes, and weak community engagement. WHO recommends aligning budgets,
governance, and information systems so that primary care is not treated as a
residual service after hospital investment.
