WHO guidance excerpt — Universal health coverage (educational sample for RAG portfolio)

Universal health coverage (UHC) means that all people have access to the full range
of quality health services they need, when and where they need them, without
financial hardship. It covers the full continuum of essential health services, from
health promotion to prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and palliative care.

UHC is measured along three dimensions often visualized as a cube: the population
covered, the services covered, and the proportion of costs covered. Progress toward
UHC requires expanding each dimension while protecting people from catastrophic
health expenditure. Out-of-pocket payments are a major cause of impoverishment
linked to health care in many countries.

Primary health care (PHC) is the cornerstone of UHC. WHO recommends reorienting
health systems toward PHC to improve equity, efficiency, and resilience. Essential
public health functions — surveillance, preparedness, health promotion, and
regulation — must be financed and governed alongside clinical services.

To advance UHC, countries should strengthen domestic public financing, reduce
reliance on out-of-pocket spending, invest in health and care workers, improve
data systems for monitoring service coverage and financial protection, and engage
communities in priority setting. Leaving no one behind requires attention to
refugees, migrants, people with disabilities, and other groups facing barriers.
