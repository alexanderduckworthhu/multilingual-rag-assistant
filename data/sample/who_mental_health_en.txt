WHO guidance excerpt: Mental health (educational sample for RAG portfolio)

Mental health is a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the
stresses of life, realize their abilities, learn well and work well, and contribute
to their community. Mental health is an integral component of health and well-being
and a basic human right. Mental health is more than the absence of mental disorders.

Determinants of mental health include individual psychological and biological factors,
as well as social, cultural, economic, political and environmental factors such as
national policies, social protection, living standards, working conditions, and
community social support. Risks can occur at all stages of life, but those that
occur during sensitive periods of development, especially early childhood, are
particularly damaging.

Promotion and prevention interventions include supporting parents and early childhood
development, schools-based programmes, workplace mental health, and community
protection for people in humanitarian emergencies. WHO recommends integrating mental
health into primary health care so that people can access services close to home
without stigma-related barriers.

In emergencies, WHO emphasizes psychological first aid, continuity of essential
psychotropic medicines, and protecting people with severe mental health conditions
from neglect and abuse. Stigma and discrimination remain major barriers to care
worldwide. Strengthening the mental health workforce and financing are priority
actions for health systems.
